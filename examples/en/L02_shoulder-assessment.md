# L02 Shoulder assessment and early loading

> This note is a verbatim transcription of the course material. Nothing has been checked, evaluated, or added.

## One-line summary
How the rotator cuff centres the humeral head, how to assess it, and how to load it in the first six weeks.

## Walkthrough

### Opening %%00:00–00:12%%
(Greeting, no course content.) Today the topic is the shoulder and how the rotator cuff keeps the humeral head centred.

### The four cuff muscles as a force couple %%00:12–00:55%%
The four rotator cuff muscles are supraspinatus, infraspinatus, teres minor and subscapularis, and they work as a force couple. When the deltoid pulls the arm up, the cuff pulls the head down and in, otherwise the head migrates superiorly and hits the acromion.

### Superior migration and impingement %%00:55–01:20%%
That superior migration is one mechanism behind subacromial impingement, but it is not the only one, so be careful with the label.

### Assessment: range, strength, special tests %%01:20–02:50%%
Assessment starts with active range of motion in flexion, abduction and external rotation, noting the painful arc between sixty and one hundred twenty degrees. Then manual muscle testing, grade zero to five; external rotation at the side is tested first because it is the least provocative. The empty can test loads supraspinatus; a positive test is pain or weakness, and the sensitivity in the literature is around seventy to eighty percent.

### Loading by phase %%02:50–04:40%%
In the acute phase, the first one to two weeks, isometrics are kept at twenty to thirty percent of maximum, five seconds hold, ten repetitions. From week three the course progresses to isotonic external rotation with a band, three sets of fifteen, RPE around six, stopping if pain goes above three out of ten. Week six onwards closed chain loading is added, like the wall push-up plus, and this is where serratus anterior really starts to matter.

### Closing summary and next lesson %%04:40–05:45%%
To summarise: centre the head, restore rotation range, load the cuff progressively, then integrate the scapula. Next lesson covers the frozen shoulder protocol.
